# Great Britain: Advanced (B2 → C1): course plan (draft)

The final course in the series. It has 12 lessons in 4 units of 3, about 30 slides each, and covers Haruka's third year in London, September to July. **The whole story wraps up at the end.**

**What the course is for:** functional English for work and life at B2–C1, with the same tone as the first two courses (warm, funny, real British life, banter). There's more business English (meetings, feedback, presentations, negotiation, business trips), and more advanced life admin.

**What drives the story:** a big work project. Haruka's team pitches to rebrand a famous (fictional) British tea company. Light puzzle elements run through it: decoding what the very British client *really* means, and a small historical puzzle in the company's archive that becomes the winning idea.

---

## 1. Story overview

**Year three.** Haruka is now the design team lead. Her English is confident and natural (she uses British phrases without thinking), but she's still Haruka: thoughtful, a bit of a perfectionist, and she still photographs every meal. Yuki has been in London a year. She's settled and funnier, and more direct than she used to be.

**Josh, the new character:** 31, a designer from Sydney, talented and very direct. He means well, but his directness is his own personality, not his nationality: Australians and Brits share most of the language, the sarcasm and the polite code. It clashes with Haruka's careful, consensus-building style and with a very reserved client. Josh's Australian side shows in light, accurate details (slang like "legend", Sydney comparisons), never as not understanding British English. The rivalry comes from communication differences, not from competing for the job. By the end they've learned from each other: Josh learns to soften, and Haruka learns to say no clearly.

**The project:** **Hartley’s Tea**, a 100-year-old (fictional) London tea company, wants a new brand to launch in Japan. It's perfect for Haruka's team, and full of tea callbacks. The client contact, **Mrs Hartley**, is the founder’s great-granddaughter. She's polite, warm and almost impossible to read ("That’s… brave." "Interesting choice." "I’ll bear it in mind.").

**The light puzzles:**

1. **Decoding the client.** In most lessons there's one short British-indirect message from Mrs Hartley. The team (and the student) work out what she really means. This is useful real-world skill, and it's what causes the misunderstandings with Josh.
2. **The archive puzzle (Units 3–4).** In the Hartley’s archive, the team finds clues on old tea tins and in letters showing that the founder, **Edith Hartley**, had a 1920s connection with a tea grower in Japan. Piecing it together gives them the winning idea for the pitch. It's fair play: every clue is shown on a slide before the answer.

**Life thread:**
- **Tom** has a girlfriend, **Amy**. The student meets her in a real scene first (Tom brings her to one of Gwen’s Sunday roasts in Unit 1, and Gwen is delighted), never through a sudden “by the way” line. They get engaged around Christmas, Tom asks Haruka to give a speech, and the wedding is the finale.
- **Mum's** *Milk Second* book comes out in the UK.
- **Gwen** is still Gwen.

**Ending (Lesson 12, July):** the pitch is won, Tom’s wedding is the final scene, Haruka gives a speech, and Mum’s book launches. Haruka decides London is home for the long term. It's a proper ending for the series.

## 4. Lessons

### Unit 1: New team

**Lesson 1: The new guy** (September)
- **Story:** Josh’s first day. Haruka runs her first team meeting as lead. Josh says "That design doesn’t work" in front of everyone, and Haruka (and Yuki) are shocked. Priya announces the Hartley’s project.
- **Practical topic:** running a meeting: an agenda, turn-taking, interrupting politely, action points.
- **Language:** softening and hedging ("I’m not sure that quite works", "Could we maybe…?").
- **Puzzle:** none yet. Set up Mrs Hartley’s first email.

**Lesson 2: What she really means** (October)
- **Story:** the first client meeting at Hartley’s. Mrs Hartley says "That’s a very brave idea" about Josh’s concept. Josh thinks it’s praise, and Haruka knows it isn’t.
- **Practical topic:** British business indirectness, decoding feedback, writing a follow-up email.
- **Language:** reporting verbs (she suggested…, she insisted…, she warned us…).
- **Puzzle:** the first "decode the client" slide.

**Lesson 3: Payday** (late October into November) · built
- **Story (work):** Josh’s first payslip has tax code 0T (no starter checklist). Haruka explains it; Priya gets payroll to fix it. Josh wants to opt out of the pension and compares it with the 12% his employer paid in Australia.
- **Story (life thread):** Tom brings Amy to Gwen’s Sunday lunch for the first time. Haruka shows Gwen the old tins from the brief: none is her nan’s. Hers had a white bird with long legs and a red cap (a crane: clue, not named). The tin is lost in the loft.
- **Hook:** Mrs Hartley says both new options are “much closer” and wants research in Japan first. She doesn’t remember a bird; the archive is “being catalogued, perhaps in the spring”. Priya sends Haruka and Josh to Tokyo and Osaka in December.
- **Practical topic:** payslips (tax code, National Insurance, workplace pension auto-enrolment).
- **Language:** causative have / get ("I had my tax code checked") and passive review.
- **Progress check:** Unit 1.

### Unit 2: Going east

**Lesson 4: Planning the trip** (late November into December) · built
- **Story:** Priya confirms a week in Japan: a day of focus groups and a department-store meeting in Tokyo, then Osaka on Thursday. Hartley’s is paying; Mrs Hartley’s note: “Economy is perfectly comfortable, I find.” Josh meets Mum on a video call and is invited for okonomiyaki. At Heathrow, Josh panics about a visa (Australians don’t need one for up to 90 days). Mum texts: big news on Thursday.
- **Practical topic:** business trip logistics, expenses and receipts, jet lag (reading).
- **Language:** future continuous (plans, in progress, polite questions: Will you be needing…?) and future perfect continuous (By Friday, I’ll have been eating sushi for five days).
- **Puzzle:** decode Mrs Hartley’s note about expenses.

**Lesson 5: Tokyo meetings** (December, the week in Japan) · built
- **Story:** The focus groups love the history, not the new look. At the department store, Josh asks buyer Ms Kato (lived in London) for a decision straight away; Haruka steps in: “What Josh means is…”. Ms Kato: “We’ll consider it carefully”, which turns out to mean “not yet” (her voicemail asks for a small April trial with a smaller tin). In Osaka, Mum’s news: Milk Second comes out in London in July. Over tea, Haruka sees cranes on Grandma’s cups and realises Gwen’s bird is a crane. On the way home Josh says Haruka spoke for him in front of the client (sets up Lesson 6).
- **Practical topic:** cross-cultural meetings, mediating, reporting back.
- **Language:** cleft sentences (What they loved was…, It’s the price that…).
- **Puzzle:** the bird is a crane. Why was a Japanese bird on a 1920s London tea tin?

**Lesson 6: Feedback** (January into February) · built
- **Story:** Haruka calls Josh’s gold designs “interesting” (her own Mrs Hartley moment); Josh hears a yes and loses a week. At Friday’s review he says, in front of Priya, “You never say what you actually think.” Priya sends them to a one-to-one: Haruka gives feedback on the Ms Kato meeting, Josh on “interesting”, and they clear the air. Tom and Amy announce their engagement (Christmas Day) and ask Haruka to give a speech at the July wedding. Gwen sees the crane photo: “That’s the bird on my nan’s tin!” Mrs Hartley: “How very curious. I shall speak to our archivist.”
- **Practical topic:** giving and receiving feedback, one-to-ones and appraisals, the SBI model.
- **Language:** mixed conditionals.
- **Progress check:** Unit 2.

### Unit 3: Pressure

**Lesson 7: Back to the drawing board** (March) · built
- **Story:** Mrs Hartley calls the one-colour design “interesting”: “A tin should tell a story.” The team speculates (Yuki: “She can’t possibly have hated it”; Priya: “I needn’t have bothered” with the printing quotes). Mrs Hartley’s voicemail: she’d rather wait for the right idea. Josh calls Ms Kato and politely postpones the trial to June (“Have you been taking lessons?”). In the archive, Ruth (archivist) shows them “Hartley’s Crane Blend”: Gwen’s crane, with faded Japanese writing Haruka reads as “Uji” and the name “Morita”. Orders stop in 1939; there are unopened boxes of letters.
- **Practical topic:** handling a setback, postponing a deadline.
- **Language:** modal perfects: needn’t have, might well have, can’t possibly have (must/can’t/should have were intermediate).
- **Puzzle:** clue 1, the Crane Blend tin (Uji, Morita, 1939).
- **Reading:** tea rationing in wartime Britain (1940–1952).

**Lesson 8: The archive** (April) · built
- **Story:** Ruth, Yuki, Josh and Haruka open boxes of letters with Japanese stamps, addressed to Edith Hartley (founder, 1925; Mrs Hartley’s great-grandmother), from K. Morita in Uji. Clue 2, Edith’s 1923 letter: the white crane is the Morita family’s symbol (“a long life and a long friendship”; “cranes fly a long way”). Clue 3, Morita’s last letter, 1939: “I hope one day the cranes will fly again.” Mrs Hartley asks them to keep it under wraps until the June pitch. Clue 4, Gwen: her nan worked in Hartley’s packing room; on the last day Edith gave every girl a crane tin and said “One day, the cranes will come back.” The idea: bring Crane Blend back, in Japan, a hundred years later; the Morita farm is believed to be run by the great-grandson. Hook: Josh’s ceiling is leaking.
- **Practical topic:** researching and summarising sources.
- **Language:** participle clauses; reporting passives.
- **Reading:** family history records (census, the 1939 Register).

**Lesson 9: Life admin** (late April) · built
- **Story:** Josh’s ceiling leaks through the light fitting and his landlord, Mr Carter, ignores him. Josh wants to write “Fix my ceiling, mate”; Haruka helps him write a formal, firm letter instead (evidence, a 7-day deadline, the council). Mr Carter replies the next day: “I’ll have a plumber round on Thursday. No need to get the council involved.” Josh finds out he can exchange his Australian licence without a test (Yuki did the same with her Japanese one). The Morita family replies: Daichi Morita kept Edith’s letters too, still paints a crane on every tea chest, and will join the pitch on a video call.
- **Practical topics:** tenants’ repair rights in England, formal complaints, exchanging a foreign driving licence.
- **Language:** inversion for emphasis (Not only has…, At no point have…, Should…).
- **Progress check:** Unit 3.

### Unit 4: The pitch

**Lesson 10: Rehearsal** (May into June) · built
- **Story:** Haruka’s first run-through is a mess; Josh: “Can I stop you there? I got lost after slide three.” Haruka takes it calmly (“Fair enough. What would help?”). Take two: a story about “two families and a white bird”, with signposts. Daichi Morita confirms he’ll join on Wednesday 14 June. Amy invites Haruka to her hen do in Bath, the weekend before the pitch. Josh admits he’s terrified too, and shares his system (breathe, pause, find a friendly face). Hook: Mrs Hartley is bringing two board members, so be ready to negotiate.
- **Practical topic:** structuring a presentation, signposting, nerves.
- **Language:** signposting and discourse markers (First…, Moving on to…, Having said that…, To sum up…).
- **Culture:** hen and stag dos.

**Lesson 11: The pitch** (Wednesday 14 June) · built
- **Story:** Haruka tells Edith’s story; Daichi Morita joins on video from the same hillside in Uji. Board member Mr Bell (Robert) questions the budget; Priya makes a conditional offer, Josh proposes meeting halfway (half now, half after the Tokyo trial), Daichi’s only deal-breaker is that the crane stays on the tin. Mrs Hartley, lost for words, says Edith had “a friend in the East” and wants to meet Gwen. Her voicemail: “The answer is yes. Crane Blend is coming home.” At the pub, Josh and Haruka look back (“brave”, “interesting”). Hook: July brings Mum’s book launch and Tom’s wedding.
- **Practical topic:** presenting, answering questions, negotiating.
- **Language:** conditional offers, provided / as long as, soft proposals (What if we…?).
- **Reading:** Getting to Yes and BATNA.

**Lesson 12: The wedding** (July) · built
- **Story:** At Mum’s book launch, Gwen gives Mrs Hartley the crane tin (“Keep it. It’s come home”), and it will go to the Tokyo launch. Haruka tells Mum London is home; Mum will visit every summer “for research”. Wedding day: Tom is late (his bus broke down); Callum is best man. Callum’s short speech, then Haruka’s (“What I didn’t know was that I’d just met my best friend in London”). Mum draws the next episode, and this time Haruka says yes. Final scene: Gwen’s kitchen, “Milk second, of course… To the next chapter.” Epilogue: Crane Blend goes on sale in Tokyo with Gwen’s tin in the window; Yuki’s crane becomes the logo; Milk Second Volume 2 ends with Haruka at home in London.
- **Practical topic:** British weddings, speeches and toasts.
- **Language:** review of emphasis for speeches (cleft sentences, inversion) plus emphatic do.
- **Progress check:** final, whole course (the student retells Haruka’s third year).

## 5. Notes for building

- Follow everything learned in the first two courses:
  - fair-play clues
  - no Japanese-only scenes in English
  - no re-teaching
  - grammar not bolded before it's taught
  - British phrases slides placed after their dialogues
  - role plays relevant and not repetitive
  - flashbacks only replay scenes the student has seen
  - no "Ha!"
  - one-line scene sentences
- Josh's directness is personal and comes from his workplace background. Don't present it as "all Australians are like this", and keep Japanese and British styles equally non-stereotyped.
- Haruka and Yuki's English is visibly better than in the intermediate course: natural British phrases, longer sentences. Their personalities stay the same.
- Facts to check before writing:
  - payslip details (tax code, NI, auto-enrolment)
  - licence exchange rules for Japan and Australia
  - Japan entry rules for Australians
  - UK wedding traditions
  - tenants’ repair rights
  - business trip expense norms
