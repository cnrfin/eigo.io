# Great Britain: Intermediate (B1 → B2): course plan (v2)

The sequel to *Great Britain for Beginners*. It has 12 lessons in 4 units of 3, and covers Haruka's second year in London, September to June.

**What the course is for:** living in Britain, not only visiting it. Most lessons pair a **practical topic** (health, renting, the Post Office, travel problems, paperwork, complaints) with a bit of **culture**, inside the story. The *Milk Second* mystery runs in the background. Most lessons give it 1 to 3 slides, and some have none.

**Not repeated from the beginner course** (checked lesson by lesson):

- cream tea and the scone debate
- Christmas and crackers
- "sorry" culture
- small talk about the weather
- accents and dialect words
- pub ordering and tipping at the bar
- the Tube and directions
- the ETA

Where a beginner topic comes back, it's a deliberate callback at a higher level (e.g. sarcasm → banter), not a repeat.

**Slides:** up to about 30 per lesson, only where the content needs it. Extra slides come from longer readings, a *Milk Second* panel, the Clue board, a British vs American "Spot the mistake" slide, or a second discussion.

## 1. Story overview

**Year two.** Haruka stayed. She feels at home now, and she's the one explaining Britain.

**Yuki, the new character:** 29, from Tokyo, a new illustrator at Haruka's company. She is always sketching, a night owl, works late for Tokyo clients, and uses some American English from school.

**Why they speak English:** Yuki and Haruka speak English at work and with British friends, so everyone is included. A teacher note explains this, as with Kenta in the beginner course.

**Milk Second:** an anonymous web comic about "Hana", a Japanese designer in London. It retells Haruka's first year: the big suitcase, brown sauce, "making a toast", and Gwen's tea rule.

- Beginner students recognise every episode.
- New students get a "previously on…".

**The artist is Haruka's mum.**

- Her English class in Osaka asked her to keep a diary in English. She loved manga as a girl, so she drew one instead.
- Her teacher suggested putting it online.

**The red herring is Yuki.** Most real clues fit her better than Mum.

**The other thread is the visa.** Haruka's Youth Mobility visa ends after year two, with no extension for Japanese nationals. Her company sponsors a Skilled Worker visa, which can be switched to from inside the UK. It's approved in Lesson 12.

**Ending:** Mum comes to London, meets Gwen… and puts the milk in first. A B2 hook: a publisher emails about a *Milk Second* book.

### Banter: a thread through the year

In year two Haruka and Tom are close friends, so Tom teases her more, and she has learned to tease back. This builds on beginner Lesson 5 (sarcasm) and teaches something central to British friendship: teasing someone is often a sign you like them. The course also admits this is a bit strange, and many British people say so themselves.

| Lesson | How it appears |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tom: "Oh look, it's Miss Tea Round." Haruka: "You're on time, Tom? Are you ill?" Yuki is shocked: "Are you two fighting?" Haruka explains, the way Gwen once explained "lovely weather". Haruka adds that friends in Osaka tease each other too, *tsukkomi*-style. That's a real link to Japan, not a stereotype, and it's Haruka's view, not the course's. |
| 6 | Callum and Tom trade England vs Scotland jokes over the bill. The reading is about banter: why friends do it, why visitors find it strange or even rude, and where the line is (at work, with people you've just met, sensitive topics). **Mystery clue:** the comic draws Tom's teasing as *mean*. Whoever draws it doesn't understand British banter, so it isn't Priya or Callum. |
| 8 | After the wrong accusation, Gwen points out the difference: "Teasing is fine. Accusing isn't." She explains how British friends make up after a real argument. |
| 12 | Gwen: "Milk first? In my house?" Mum, in English: "In my comic, Gwen, always milk first." Mum has learned to tease back. |

**Coverage check against the Core Inventory:**

- **B1 grammar covered:** present perfect continuous, present perfect vs past simple, past perfect, will and going to for prediction (Lesson 10), 2nd and 3rd conditionals, passive, reported speech, deduction with must and can't, should have and might have, complex question tags, would for past habit (Lesson 12), need to (Lesson 2), too and enough (Lesson 7), and extended phrasal verbs (spread across lessons).
- **B2 items introduced:** wish, past perfect continuous, mixed conditionals (for recognition), relative clauses, and future perfect.
- **B1 functions covered:** checking understanding, describing experiences and feelings, opinions, agreeing and disagreeing, starting and ending conversations, managing interaction, complaints, and "less routine situations" (lost luggage, a parcel abroad, train compensation).

## 5. Lessons

Each lesson has four dialogues, a listening page after dialogue 2, a Watch and describe video, a reading, and a pronunciation point that differs from all beginner points.

### Unit 1: Year two

**Lesson 1: The new girl** (September)

- **Story:**
  - Yuki's first day. Haruka shows her round the office (personal mugs, the kitchen).
  - Haruka shows her the tea round and takes her to Greggs.
  - Tom turns up late and teases Haruka; she teases back; Yuki: "Are you two fighting?"
- **Practical topic:** office life. Tea rounds, after-work drinks, and what colleagues mean ("Not bad" = good, "I'll bear it in mind" = probably not).
- **Can-do:** Students can start and keep up a conversation with someone new and give them useful advice.
- **Language:** present perfect continuous ("I've been living here for a year"). Advice: "If I were you…" (as a phrase), "You ought to…".
- **Culture:**
  - A short slide: teasing means we're friends (the banter thread starts).
  - Reading: Greggs. Founded by John Gregg in Gosforth, Newcastle, in 1939. The 2019 vegan sausage roll led to record sales. Now about 2,500 shops.
- **Listening:** a colleague's voicemail: "Whose turn is the tea round?"
- **Pronunciation:** weak "been" /bɪn/.
- **Estimated slides:** about 28.

**Lesson 2: Flat hunting** (October)

- **Story:**
  - Yuki's short-term room ends. Haruka goes with her to two flat viewings: one too good to be true ("Just send the deposit first…"), one small but real.
  - They furnish it from charity shops.
  - At lunch Priya shows them a comic: "She looks just like you, Haruka!"
- **Practical topic:** renting.
  - Viewings, deposits, "bills included".
  - Council tax (and the 25% discount if you live alone).
  - Bin day.
  - Spotting a scam: never pay before you've seen the flat.
- **Can-do:** Students can ask detailed questions about a flat or service and understand the answers.
- **Language:** obligation and necessity at B1 ("You need to…", "You don't need to…", "You're supposed to…", "You must never…").
- **Culture:** charity shops (a short slide).
- **Reading:** renting in the UK: what to check before you sign.
- **Listening:** a letting agent's voicemail.
- **Pronunciation:** word stress in compound nouns (**COUN**cil tax, **BIN** day, **LET**ting agent).
- **Mystery:** a panel from Episode 1, the big suitcase.
- **Estimated slides:** about 29.

**Lesson 3: Under the weather** (November)

- **Story:**
  - Haruka gets a bad cold that won't go.
  - Gwen's cure is tea. Tom's "advice" is teasing ("Have you tried moaning about it more? Works for me.").
  - She calls NHS 111 and registers with a GP.
  - Episode 3 is brown sauce; Priya says her account is public, so Haruka makes it private… and the next episode still shows that week's meals.
- **Practical topic:** health.
  - Registering with a GP: free, no proof of address or ID needed in England.
  - NHS 111, pharmacists.
  - 999, 111 or 101?
- **Can-do:** Students can describe symptoms, explain how long a problem has lasted, and understand simple advice.
- **Language:** present perfect vs past simple ("I've had it since Monday", "It started on Monday").
- **Culture:** poppies at work in November (a short scene).
- **Reading:** "What to do when you get ill in the UK", compared with walk-in clinics in Japan.
- **Listening:** a GP surgery's automated phone message.
- **Pronunciation:** /ɪ/ vs /iː/ (ill, feel, sick, sleep).
- **Mystery:** the first deduction: it's someone in her private circle.
- **Progress check:** Unit 1.
- **Estimated slides:** about 30.

### Unit 2: Home and away

**Lesson 4: The Christmas queue** (December)

- **Story:**
  - Haruka and Yuki queue at the Post Office to send presents to Japan before the last posting date. They need a customs form (CN22) and have to decide whether to send it tracked.
  - Gwen takes everyone to a panto.
  - *Milk Second* goes viral, and Gwen is thrilled.
  - The 3 a.m. posting time comes up. Yuki: "I'm always up then!"
- **Practical topic:** the Post Office. Sending a parcel abroad, customs forms for gifts, tracked post, last posting dates.
- **Can-do:** Students can ask for detailed information politely and check they've understood.
- **Language:** indirect questions ("Could you tell me how long it takes?"); question tags for checking ("It's £20, isn't it?").
- **Culture:** pantomime ("Oh no it isn't!", the dame).
- **Reading:** panto.
- **Listening:** the Post Office queue announcement ("Cashier number four, please").
- **Pronunciation:** question-tag intonation (checking vs really asking).
- **Mystery:** the posting-time clue.
- **Estimated slides:** about 29.

**Lesson 5: Lost in arrivals** (January)

- **Story:**
  - New Year in Osaka. Haruka explains Japanese New Year to Tom on a video call; this time Haruka is the expert.
  - Kenta is suspected and cleared.
  - Mum is tired and hiding her tablet, with an English textbook on the table.
  - Back at Heathrow, Haruka's suitcase doesn't arrive. Yes, the same big suitcase. She reports it at the baggage desk.
- **Practical topic:** lost luggage. Report it before leaving the baggage hall, fill in a Property Irregularity Report (PIR), describe the bag, follow up. After 21 days it counts as lost.
- **Can-do:** Students can report a problem and tell the story of what happened in order.
- **Language:** narrative tenses and past perfect ("By the time I got to the carousel, everyone had gone").
- **Culture:** Japanese New Year through British eyes (osechi, hatsumōde, nengajō), with the student explaining.
- **Reading:** "My suitcase is missing! What now?"
- **Listening:** a Heathrow announcement about baggage.
- **Pronunciation:** past perfect contractions ("I'd already…", "she'd gone").
- **Mystery:** dramatic irony in Osaka; Kenta is ruled out.
- **Teacher note:** at home they speak Japanese; the dialogues are in English for practice.
- **Estimated slides:** about 29.

**Lesson 6: Who's paying?** (25 January)

- **Story:**
  - Callum organises a Burns Night dinner at a Scottish restaurant: haggis, neeps and tatties, a poem read to the haggis.
  - Callum and Tom trade England vs Scotland jokes.
  - Tunnock's teacakes come with coffee.
  - The bill: split it or pay for what you had? Is service included?
  - The Burns Night episode has American English, and Tom's teasing is drawn as mean.
- **Practical topic:** restaurants and money.
  - Splitting the bill.
  - Discretionary service charge (often 12.5% in London; you can ask to remove it; since October 2024 staff must get all of it).
  - Tipping at restaurants vs the pub (the beginner course covered the pub).
- **Can-do:** Students can deal with the bill in a group and explain a tradition or how something is made.
- **Language:** passives ("Haggis is made from…", "Service isn't included", "The poem was written in 1786").
- **Culture:**
  - Burns Night (a short slide).
  - Tunnock's: Uddingston, near Glasgow; the teacake was first made in 1956.
- **Reading:** banter: why British friends tease each other, why it can seem strange, and where the line is.
- **Listening:** the waiter: "Would you like to split it?"
- **Pronunciation:** sarcastic vs sincere intonation ("Oh, *brilliant*").
- **Mystery:** a "Spot the mistake" slide on British vs American English, and the banter clue.
- **Progress check:** Unit 2.
- **Estimated slides:** about 30.

### Unit 3: Getting closer

**Lesson 7: Delays on the line** (February)

- **Story:**
  - A pancake race at work on Shrove Tuesday (one short scene).
  - Haruka and Gwen book trains to Cardiff for St David's Day, and the train is cancelled. Rail replacement bus. They arrive two hours late.
  - On the bus, Haruka and Tom (by text) build the clue board. Everything points to Yuki.
- **Practical topic:** train problems.
  - Advance vs off-peak tickets.
  - Cancellations and strikes, rail replacement buses.
  - Claiming Delay Repay: depends on the delay; claim from the train company within 28 days.
- **Can-do:** Students can speculate about a situation from evidence and deal with travel disruption.
- **Language:**
  - Modals of deduction, present ("It must be someone at work", "It can't be Tom", "The train might be…").
  - too and enough.
- **Reading:** "Your train's late. Claim your money back."
- **Listening:** a train announcement: "We're sorry to announce…"
- **Pronunciation:** "must be" and "can't be": weak t and linking.
- **Mystery:** the main Clue board.
- **Estimated slides:** about 29.

**Lesson 8: St David's Day** (1 March)

- **Story:**
  - In Cardiff, Haruka gets a message from Yuki and accuses her by text. She's wrong: the sketchbook is Yuki's comic-con portfolio. Yuki is hurt.
  - Gwen and Sian help her see it ("Teasing is fine. Accusing isn't.").
  - Daffodils, Welsh cakes, bilingual signs.
  - Back in London, Haruka apologises properly and Yuki becomes her ally.
- **Practical topic:** repairing a friendship. Admitting you were wrong, explaining, making up, at B1 level. The beginner course taught everyday "sorry".
- **Can-do:** Students can apologise for a serious mistake, explain what happened and make up with someone.
- **Language:**
  - Past deduction ("She must have…", "She can't have…").
  - Regret ("I should have asked her first").
- **Culture:** St David's Day and the Welsh language (bilingual signs, place names).
- **Reading:** Wales and the Welsh language today.
- **Listening:** a bilingual Welsh–English station announcement.
- **Pronunciation:** "should have" → /ʃʊdəv/; Welsh "ll".
- **Mystery:** Yuki ruled out.
- **Estimated slides:** about 30.

**Lesson 9: Paperwork** (March)

- **Story:**
  - Priya says the company will sponsor Haruka's visa, and the paperwork begins: a formal phone call, a formal email, documents.
  - Haruka finds out UK Mother's Day is in March and posts Mum a card.
  - Mum's call has more English in it.
  - The next episode shows the card opened from the receiver's point of view. Haruka is too busy to notice.
- **Practical topic:** formal phone calls and emails. Register (formal vs friendly), standard phrases, checking and passing on information.
- **Can-do:** Students can pass on what someone said and handle a simple formal call or email.
- **Language:** reported speech across tenses ("They said I'd need…", "Priya told me the company would…").
- **Culture:** Mothering Sunday vs Mother's Day in Japan (May).
- **Reading:** "Writing emails in British English: Dear, Hi or Hiya?"
- **Listening:** an automated call ("Your call is important to us…").
- **Pronunciation:** polite formal intonation on the phone.
- **Mystery:** the decisive clue.
- **Progress check:** Unit 3.
- **Estimated slides:** about 29.

### Unit 4: Milk Second

**Lesson 10: Beside the seaside** (May bank holiday)

- **Story:**
  - A trip to Brighton with Tom and Yuki: the pier, seagulls stealing chips (Tom's teasing).
  - The hotel has given away their room.
  - The news: the *Milk Second* artist will appear at MCM Comic Con London.
  - Mum is going on "a trip with friends".
- **Practical topic:** making a complaint politely but firmly, and getting a solution.
- **Can-do:** Students can make a complaint and say what would or would have happened in other situations.
- **Language:**
  - 2nd and 3rd conditionals ("If we'd booked directly, we'd have…").
  - will and going to for prediction.
  - Mixed conditionals for recognition only.
- **Reading:** the British seaside (piers, bank holidays).
- **Listening:** the hotel's apology.
- **Pronunciation:** "If I'd known" and "would have" /wʊdəv/.
- **Mystery:** the timing clue.
- **Estimated slides:** about 28.

**Lesson 11: Comic Con** (late May)

- **Story:**
  - Tickets, the queue and the bag check at ExCeL; Yuki in cosplay.
  - The *Milk Second* artist walks on stage, and it's Mum.
  - Afterwards they have an emotional talk, and Mum explains in English.
- **Practical topic:** big events. Tickets, entry times, bag rules, finding people in a crowd.
- **Can-do:** Students can express strong emotions and explain why something mattered to them.
- **Language:** past perfect continuous ("She'd been drawing it for months"); wish ("I wish you'd told me").
- **Culture:** MCM Comic Con London (ExCeL, usually the last weekend of May and October); manga in the UK.
- **Reading:** manga in the UK.
- **Listening:** the host introducing the artist.
- **Pronunciation:** emotional and contrastive stress ("It was YOU?").
- **Mystery:** the reveal.
- **Estimated slides:** about 30.

**Lesson 12: Milk first** (June)

- **Story:**
  - Haruka shows Mum around London: contactless, the bus, what to see.
  - Tea at Gwen's: Mum puts the milk in first, Gwen teases her, and Mum teases back.
  - The visa is approved. Haruka gives a short thank-you speech.
  - Hook: a publisher emails about a *Milk Second* book.
- **Practical topic:** hosting a visitor (plans, recommendations, tips).
- **Can-do:** Students can sum up a year of experiences and talk about future plans and hopes.
- **Language:**
  - Relative clauses ("Gwen, who has very strict tea rules…").
  - Future perfect ("By next year I'll have…").
  - used to and would ("I used to think…").
- **Reading:** Haruka's blog, "What I wish I'd known about living in the UK". It brings back every practical topic.
- **Listening:** Gwen's voicemail: "Bring your mum. I'll put the kettle on."
- **Pronunciation:** pausing and chunking in a short speech.
- **Mystery:** the pay-off.
- **Progress check:** final.
- **Estimated slides:** about 28.

## 6. Notes for building

- **Story flow:** every practical topic comes out of something that happens to a character:
  - Yuki needs a flat.
  - Haruka gets ill.
  - Presents have to reach Japan.
  - The suitcase goes missing.
  - Someone has to pay the bill.
  - A train is cancelled.
  - The visa needs paperwork.
  - A hotel gets it wrong.
  - A ticketed event.
  - Mum visits.

  No topic is "taught" without a reason in the story.
- **Japanese learners at B1:**
  - Extending answers.
  - Fillers.
  - Indirect questions as politeness.
  - Responding to teasing (it's OK not to tease back at first; "Very funny, Tom" is enough).
  - Katakana false friends in renting (mansion = flat) and health.
- **Banter, carefully:** present it as something many British friends do, not all, and say clearly it can feel rude or confusing. Include where the line is. Haruka's Osaka comparison is her view, offered as a discussion question, not a claim about Japan.
- **Facts to check before writing each lesson:**
  - Greggs figures.
  - Council tax rules (and London borough differences).
  - Deposit protection.
  - GP and NHS 111 steps (England).
  - Royal Mail to Japan (CN22 up to £270, last posting dates for the story year).
  - PIR and the 21-day rule.
  - Service charge law.
  - Delay Repay bands.
  - Skilled Worker rules (eligible occupation, salary).
  - MCM dates for the story year.
  - Mothering Sunday date for the story year (7 March in 2027).
- **Topics left over:** returning faulty goods and consumer rights. This could replace the event topic in Lesson 11, or go in a B2 course.
- **Beginner course:** no changes needed. Greggs and Tunnock's fit here.
