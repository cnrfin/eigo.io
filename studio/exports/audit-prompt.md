---

You are reviewing an English course before it is published. Be a strict, independent reviewer. Do not rewrite the course; report problems.

## About the course

- **Course:** Great Britain for Beginners, CEFR A2. 12 lessons of about 30 minutes, in 4 units.
- **Learners:** adult Japanese learners in 1:1 online lessons with a British teacher. The slides sit beside the video call.
- **Story:** Haruka, 26, from Osaka, spends a working-holiday year in London. Other characters:
  - Gwen, her Welsh landlady
  - Tom, her flatmate from Manchester
  - Priya, a colleague from Birmingham
  - Callum, from Edinburgh
  - Kenta, Haruka's brother
  - Linda, Tom's mum
- **Lesson shape:** each lesson has 4 dialogues, vocabulary, language focus and practice, pronunciation, role plays, a reading and a discussion.
- **House rules:**
  - British English throughout.
  - Haruka speaks clear, correct but simple English, like a real Japanese learner. British words come from the British characters.
  - Characters must not be stereotypes.
  - Every cultural fact must be true.
  - Student-facing explanations must be A2 level. Teacher notes can be harder.
- **Formatting:** text marked "(teacher only)" and "Teacher notes" is not shown to students. Lines like "[image: …]" are placeholders.

## What to check

1. **Naturalness.** Would a British person really say this line, in this situation? Flag anything stiff, odd, too American, dated or unidiomatic. Does Haruka sound like a believable Japanese learner, not a native speaker and not a caricature?
2. **Logic and continuity.**
   - Does each dialogue make sense as text only, line by line?
   - Does anything contradict earlier lessons (names, places, dates, who said what, running jokes)?
   - Is the timeline consistent (September to August)?
3. **Accuracy.** List every cultural or factual claim you think is wrong, out of date or doubtful (prices, dates, customs, history, place names), and say why.
4. **Level.**
   - Is anything student-facing clearly above A2: words, grammar, idioms?
   - Is anything too easy (A1)?
   - Check the objectives against CEFR A2 descriptors and the British Council/EAQUALS Core Inventory.
5. **Teaching effectiveness.**
   - Does each language focus match the dialogues and practice?
   - Are practice items clear and answerable, and are the answers correct?
   - Is target language recycled across lessons?
   - Are there gaps in A2 coverage?
6. **Japanese-learner fit.** Are common Japanese-learner difficulties handled well (articles, plurals, l/r, katakana English, nodding/aizuchi)? Are any comparisons with Japan inaccurate or stereotyped?
7. **Consistency.** Spelling, terminology, character names, slide titles and formatting across lessons.

## How to report

Return a table with one row per issue and these columns:

| Lesson | Slide | Quote | Problem | Category (1–7) | Severity (must fix / should fix / nice to have) | Suggested fix |

Then give:

- the 5 most important issues overall
- anything the course does especially well, briefly
- your confidence for factual claims: say "unsure" rather than guessing

Do not report personal style preferences as errors. If a line is natural British English, leave it.
