# Pronunciation course: plan

**"Understood First Time"**: eigo's pronunciation course for Japanese adults, A2 and up. It follows `design-principles.md`. Status: **agreed outline**; the next step is designing the blocks, then building lesson 4 as the pilot.

## Goal

- **The student is understood first time.** The goal is not a native accent, and lessons never promise one.
- **One fixed order for everyone**, ranked by what most often stops Japanese speakers being understood:
  1. extra vowels and stress;
  2. the confusing consonants and vowels;
  3. rhythm and tone in sentences.
- **Every lesson has one sound focus and one real situation**, and ends with the student using the sound in that situation.

- **One main sound per lesson.** A related second sound goes into the extras only (e.g. S/SH in lesson 10's extras, "-th" in dates in lesson 5's extras).
- **Each unit ends with a short challenge** in the last lesson's extras: a mixed listening set and a role-play using all three of the unit's sounds.

## Lesson blueprint (25-minute core)

| # | Stage | Min | Student does | Block |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm-up | 3 | Says their sentence from last time, then 4 quick listening items from the last lesson | Speaking prompt, Audio choice |
| 2 | Goal | 1 | Reads the can-do and sees today's situation (no separate "first try" slide: the role-play later is where they use it) | Can-do and progress |
| 3 | Hear it | 6 | Taps which word or picture they heard: single words, then in sentences, then at natural speed | Audio choice |
| 4 | How it's made | 2 | Guesses first, then sees one mouth/tongue picture, a one-line tip and the katakana contrast | Audio choice (guess) → reveal |
| 5 | Say it | 5 | Hears the model, repeats, then says it alone (6–8 words); then the "Did you get it?" game | Model and repeat, Say and match |
| 6 | Use it | 5 | Fills real phrases with their own details, then a short role-play in the situation | Personal gap-fill, Speaking prompt (role-play) |
| 7 | Wrap-up | 3 | Can-do check, one strength and one target, their score, a sentence for next time | Can-do and progress |
| E | Extras (1–3) | — | A speed race, natural-speed listening, the unit challenge | Timer, Audio choice |

- **The student speaks** in stages 1, 5 and 6 and in the stage-4 guesses: at least 60% of the lesson.
- **Keeping it engaging:**
  - the "Did you get it?" game, where a point only counts if the tutor understood the word;
  - a speed race against the timer;
  - the student's own name, job and favourite food in every role-play;
  - a score at the end ("You heard 7 of 8 correctly").
- **Kept safe:**
  - the tutor models first;
  - it waits 5–10 seconds before helping;
  - at most 3 tries per word, then it moves on;
  - praise comes before correction.

## Progress

Kept simple and familiar:

- "Lesson 4 of 14" on the first and last slide.
- **The listening score** at the end of each lesson ("You heard 7 of 8 correctly"). It comes from the tap games, so it's exact.
- **The can-do check:** ✓ / with help / not yet. The student rates themselves; the tutor can add a word of encouragement.

## The student's own phrases

Every lesson saves 2–3 of the student's own sentences to their word list: their name, job, station, favourite food. Later lessons reuse them in practice, and lesson 14's self-introduction is built from them. It's the course's payoff.

## Between lessons

Never called "homework", anywhere a student sees or hears it. Optional and light:
- the lesson's phrases are in their word list to review;
- **"a sentence for next time"** to try saying, which opens the next lesson's warm-up. If they haven't practised it, they just try it then, with no pressure.

There are no recordings, and nothing is stored.

## AI tutor and human teacher

Both teach every lesson from the same slides.

- **What the AI reports.** The AI only says two things it can actually know:
  - **What the student heard:** the tap scores, which are exact.
  - **Whether it understood the word:** "I understood *light*" / "I heard *right*. Once more?"

  It never says a sound was made correctly, and never mentions "accuracy".
- **"Understood" is generous.** The AI understands from context, so it often hears what it expects. The listening score is the reliable number; "understood" is encouragement and practice.
- **The honest note: once per lesson, at the wrap-up, never as a pitch.**
  - Example: "I can tell when I understand you, but a human teacher listens far more closely to how each sound is made."
  - The student decides for themselves whether to move to a Student Lite or Standard plan. The AI doesn't mention prices or plans.
- **A human teacher:**
  - listens to *how* the sound is made, and gives one physical tip in words;
  - can slow down or repeat a stage;
  - doesn't show answers or skip the wrap-up.

## Blocks to build (7)

| # | Block | Student sees | Tells the tutor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Audio choice** | Play button, 2–4 word or picture tiles, replay limit, score. Modes: words, pictures, "which syllable is strong", rise or fall | Item, choice, right or wrong, replays, set score |
| 2 | **Can-do and progress** | Goal, "Lesson x of 14", step bar; at the end ✓ / with help / not yet and the score | Student's rating, score, lesson finished |
| 3 | **Model and repeat** | A word or phrase card with play buttons (UK/US, male/female) and a tries counter | Item, tries (max 3), whether the tutor understood it |
| 4 | **Say and match** ("Did you get it?") | Two word tiles; the student secretly taps one, says it, and the tutor says what it heard; points. A swap mode where the tutor speaks and the student taps | Round score. **The secret pick is hidden from the AI until after it has said what it heard;** otherwise the game means nothing |
| 5 | **Speaking prompt** | Question or situation card, optional picture, sentence starters, a hidden model answer, an optional goal list for role-plays | Goals ticked, whether the target words were understood |
| 6 | **Personal gap-fill** | Phrase frames ("A table for ___, please") the student fills by speaking or typing | What they filled in; saved to their word list |
| 7 | **Timer** | Countdown bar for races and phases | Time used, items done |

Each block also needs an editor in the studio, and its state sent to the AI tutor in the slide notes. A reveal for "how it's made" uses the existing image block with a hidden-until-tapped option.

## Audio

- **Reuse the old course's clips:** minimal pairs and words in UK and US, male and female voices. Use them wherever the words match.
- **New clips** are made with the same four voices (ElevenLabs), so the whole course sounds consistent.
- **Students hear both accents.** Each word plays in one voice by default; the others are a tap away.

## Sample lesson: lesson 4, "L and R"

**Can-do:** say names and places with L and R so people get them first time.
**Situation:** booking a table and checking in under your name.
**Pairs:** light/right, glass/grass, collect/correct, fly/fry, play/pray, long/wrong, alive/arrive, cloud/crowd.
**Phrases:** "a table for ___", "under the name ___", "on the left / on the right", "Could you say that again?", "That's correct."
**Recycles:** final consonants from lesson 2 (glass, crowd); loanword stress from lesson 3 (hotel, reservation); the student's own phrases.

| # | Slide | Block | Tutor notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Goal: "Lesson 4 of 14", can-do, step bar, 「LとRを聞き分けて、言い分けよう」 | Can-do and progress | Under 30 seconds |
| 2 | "Your sentence from last time": the student says it | Speaking prompt | Say only whether you understood the loanword. Praise first. If they haven't practised, it's fine: they just try it now |
| 3 | Review: 4 "which syllable is strong?" items from lesson 3 | Audio choice (stress) | Give the score, then move on whatever it is |
| 4 | Hear it: words, 8 items (tap which word) | Audio choice | Just hearing, no rule yet. Under 6/8 → do extra E2 before the game |
| 5 | Hear it: meaning, 6 sentences with two pictures (something in the sky: a cloud or a crowd?) | Audio choice (pictures) | This checks meaning, which is the point of the course |
| 6 | How it's made: "When you say ラ, does your tongue touch the top of your mouth?" Then reveal two side-view pictures | Audio choice (guess) → reveal | Under a minute. L: tongue tip pressed behind the top teeth, and held. R: tongue pulled back, touching nothing, lips a little round. *Human teacher:* listen for a ラ-like tap. *AI:* never says the sound was made right |
| 7 | Say it: 8 words, pair by pair; model → repeat → alone | Model and repeat | AI: "I understood *collect*" or "I heard *correct*. Once more?" Max 3 tries. Pitfall: an extra vowel after a final L (use the lesson 2 tip, "stop on the consonant") |
| 8 | "Did you get it?": 8 rounds, then swap | Say and match | Misses are information, not failure. Speak at natural speed in swap mode |
| 9 | Real phrases: fill "A table for ___, please. Under the name ___." with their own details, then say them | Personal gap-fill | Romanised names use R for ラ (Ryo, Haruka), so English listeners expect an R. Ask how the student wants their name said. Save to their phrases |
| 10 | Role-play: phone booking, then checking in. Goals: name, number of people, time, left or right table | Speaking prompt (role-play) | Ask them to repeat naturally when you miss a detail. Wait 5–10 seconds before helping |
| 11 | Make it yours: "Name 2–3 places in your life with L or R: your street, station, company, a restaurant." | Speaking prompt | Ends on the student's own life. One correction at most. Save one sentence |
| 12 | Wrap-up: can-do check, one strength and one target, score, a sentence for next time ("My name is ___ and I live near ___") | Can-do and progress | The AI's honest note, once |
| E1 | Extra: speed chain. Build "a light lunch" → "…on the right" → "…on the right, please" in 20 seconds | Timer | Score = links understood |
| E2 | Extra: more hearing, 8 pairs in short sentences at natural speed | Audio choice | Required before the game if the first Hear it score was under 6/8 |
