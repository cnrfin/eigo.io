# eigo course design principles

How every eigo.io course lesson is designed. It applies to lessons taught by the AI tutor and by a human teacher. Both use the same slides; the tutor notes say where they differ.

These principles come from eigo's own teaching experience, published research on language learning (retrieval practice, spaced recycling, output over input, task-based learning), and an internal analysis of how other 1:1 courses are built. That analysis covered method only. No content from it is used. Course content is always written from scratch.

Learners: Japanese adults, mostly A2 to B1, studying in 1:1 online lessons.

## 1. Lesson shape

- **25-minute core, plus extras.** Every lesson has a core that fits 25 minutes, so it works in a 25- or 30-minute lesson. After the core come 1 to 3 "extra" slides (a game, another practice set, a challenge) that the tutor uses only if there is time. Extras are marked as extras.
- **One clear goal.** The lesson starts with a single can-do sentence ("By the end, you can…"). The wrap-up repeats it as a check: ✓ / with help / not yet.
- **The student talks most.** Aim for the student speaking at least 60% of the lesson. A slide that is only explanation lasts under a minute.
- **No reading slides aloud.** A slide is never a paragraph for the tutor to read. Explanations are a short line, a picture or a diagram, plus the tutor's spoken words. Long text is dead time, especially in a synthetic voice.

## 2. Patterns every lesson uses

1. **Try first, then reveal.** The student guesses, answers or attempts before the answer, model or rule appears.
2. **Test, then teach**, where it fits. A quick cold attempt shows the gap before the strategy or rule.
3. **From controlled to free.** Recognise → repeat → use in a sentence → use about yourself.
4. **A few items, recycled many times.** 6 to 10 target items per lesson, each met in several different activities. Depth over breadth.
5. **Make it about the student.** Every lesson ends on the student's own life, work or plans.
6. **Fade the support.** Show the pattern, then hide it, then the student uses it alone.
7. **One strength, one target.** The wrap-up names one thing the student did well and one thing to work on next.
8. **Visible progress.** The student sees where they are in the course and what changed since last time.

## 3. Rules from what doesn't work

- **Answers are never visible to the student.** They live in tutor notes or in a block that reveals them after the attempt.
- **No activity the AI can't see or hear.** Nothing that relies on pointing, thumbs, circling or watching the mouth. Use a tap, a spoken answer or a choice instead.
- **Every drill ends in real use.** Words → phrases → a sentence the student would actually say.
- **Every extra activity serves the lesson's goal.** No bolted-on idioms or trivia.
- **Promise only what the lesson practises.** If the goal mentions pronunciation, the lesson includes it.

## 4. Japanese learners

- **Hear it before saying it.** For difficult sounds, the student first learns to hear the difference, then says it.
- **Katakana English.** Vocabulary blocks flag loanwords and false friends (mansion, smart, claim, consent, tension). Pronunciation contrasts the katakana form with the English one (extra vowels, equal-weight syllables).
- **Articles and plurals.** A running thread: noticed in feedback across all courses, and taught directly in the grammar courses.
- **Thinking time.** Before open speaking, give sentence starters and time to think. The tutor waits 5 to 10 seconds before offering a hint, and never interrupts.
- **Model first.** The tutor says it first, the student repeats, then the student says it alone.
- **Praise the attempt, then correct.** Corrections follow eigo's correction rules (one at a time, at natural break points in courses).
- **Clear structure.** Show the lesson plan and a step counter. At lower levels, add a short Japanese gloss on task instructions.

## 5. AI tutor and human teacher

- **One set of slides.** Tutor notes carry the expected answers, back-up examples and follow-up questions. The AI reads them; a human teacher uses them as a guide.
- **Mark the human-only parts.** Where something needs an expert ear or eye (judging how natural an accent sounds, cultural nuance), the tutor notes mark it. The AI does the activity as practice and doesn't claim to judge it.
- **Be honest about limits.** Where it matters, the AI says plainly what a human teacher would do better, then lets the student decide. It's information, never a sales pitch.

## 6. Interactive blocks

Each block reports its state to the tutor: the student's answer, whether it was right, and how many tries. The AI tutor needs this to coach; a human teacher sees the same on screen. The full block list is in each course plan. These are the shared ones, in priority order:

1. **Reveal:** an answer, model or definition hidden until the student has tried.
2. **Choice:** pick one (meaning, form, which word you heard).
3. **Gap-fill:** from a word bank, or spoken.
4. **Speaking prompt:** a question card with an optional picture, sentence starters and a hidden model answer.
5. **Timer:** prep and speaking phases, or a race.
6. **Can-do checklist and progress:** the lesson goal, then ticks and course progress at the end.

## 7. Between lessons

Never called "homework", anywhere a student sees it or hears it: the word stresses students out. It's optional and light, through the app: the lesson's phrases are in the word list to review, plus "a sentence for next time" to try saying. The next lesson's warm-up starts with it, in a relaxed way. If the student hasn't practised, that's fine: they just try it then.

## 8. Checks before a lesson is finished

- [ ] One can-do goal, at the course's level.
- [ ] Core fits 25 minutes; extras are marked.
- [ ] The student speaks at least 60% of the time.
- [ ] No answers visible on student slides.
- [ ] No paragraph slides; every explanation is under a minute.
- [ ] Ends in real use and on the student's own life.
- [ ] Recycles at least one earlier point and 3 earlier words or items.
- [ ] Japanese-learner pitfalls are in the tutor notes.
- [ ] Human-only parts are marked.
- [ ] Wrap-up: can-do check, one strength and one target, a sentence for next time (never called homework).
